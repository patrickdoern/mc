In short

The Moonlight Commander app does not collect any personal data. It only looks for new versions if you allow it, and sends nothing about you when it does. Connections to other computers only occur when you open one yourself or share folders. This website is hosted on GitHub Pages, sets no cookies and measures its reach anonymously with Matomo on my own server.

The app

  • The app runs entirely locally on your Mac. There is no account, no cloud, no analytics, no crash reports and no advertising.
  • Otherwise, network connections only occur when you open an SFTP or FTP server or an SMB share yourself via "Network Connection". They go directly to the server you specified, not through me. SMB shares are mounted by macOS itself, as in the Finder.
  • Direct connection between two Macs: While you share folders via "Command → Share …", the app makes your Mac visible on the local network via Bonjour under the display name you set there (by default the computer name) and accepts connections. While the "Network Connection" dialog is open, the app searches the local network for such shares. When connecting, both Macs exchange their display names and a certificate created by the app. Only when you choose "Allow" on the sharing Mac (as long as the two Macs are not paired, only after both sides have shown the same code) or have set that Mac to "Always Allow" before can the other Mac read the shared folders and, only if you ticked "Allow writing" when sharing, also change and delete files in them; depending on the command and the setting on the other Mac, deleted items go to your Mac's Trash or are deleted permanently. It has no access to anything else. The data travels encrypted directly between the two Macs on the local network, not through me or any other server. Closing the share dialog stops listening immediately. The display name and the shared folders are stored locally in the settings, as are the name and certificate fingerprint of the Macs you are paired with; you can delete these under "Command → Paired Macs …".
  • On first launch, the app asks whether it may check for new versions at startup. Only if you agree does it fetch https://api.github.com/repos/patrickdoern/mc/releases/latest, at most once every 24 hours. The same happens when you choose "Check for Updates …" in the menu. No personal data is sent. Technically, GitHub inevitably sees your IP address and the user agent "Moonlight-Commander/<version>" with the app's version number; GitHub's privacy statement applies to this. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any time in the settings under "Check for updates at startup". The app downloads and installs nothing by itself; if a newer version exists, it only shows a notice with a link to the download page.
  • On request, the app stores a server password (SFTP, FTP, SMB) in your Mac's keychain. It leaves the Mac only to sign in to the respective server and can be deleted again under "Command → Saved Passwords". For the direct connection, the app creates a key and a certificate "Moonlight Commander" in the keychain on first use; the key never leaves the Mac, and the certificate is only seen by the Mac you connect to.
  • Settings such as folders, sorting, color scheme, hotlist and the history of viewed files are stored locally in your user account's preferences.

Reach measurement with Matomo

I use the software Matomo to see how often the page is visited. Matomo runs on my own server at metrics.doern.eu; there is no external service provider, and the data is not passed on to third parties. Matomo is configured to set no cookies and to immediately shorten the IP address by two bytes, so that it can no longer be attributed to a person.

The following is recorded: the page visited, the page you came from, date and time, approximate location (country and region from the shortened IP address), browser, operating system, device type, screen resolution and language, as well as clicks on outgoing links and downloads. I delete raw data after 180 days; only aggregated statistics remain. The legal basis is the legitimate interest in designing and improving the offering according to demand (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
